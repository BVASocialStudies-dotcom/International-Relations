U.S. and China: Alliances & Agreements

Click each item below to learn more about it. As you read, think about which relationships involve a military promise, and which ones don't.

US

United States

Formal alliances and defense arrangements

Military Alliance

A 32-member alliance linking the U.S., Canada, and countries across Europe. Under Article 5, an armed attack against one member is treated as an attack against all members, making it the strongest form of mutual defense commitment the U.S. holds.

Intelligence Alliance

A close intelligence-sharing partnership between the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. It began after World War II and focuses on collecting and sharing surveillance and signals intelligence rather than on military defense promises.

Security Partnership

A 2021 security partnership between Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Its centerpiece is helping Australia build a fleet of nuclear-powered submarines, along with cooperation on other advanced military technology such as artificial intelligence and cyber capabilities.

Regional Security Framework

A term used to describe how the U.S. built its Asia-Pacific alliances after World War II: through separate bilateral treaties with countries like Japan, South Korea, and the Philippines, rather than one big multilateral alliance like NATO. The U.S. sits at the "hub," with each ally as a "spoke" connected to Washington, but not necessarily to each other.

Strategic Dialogue

An informal strategic partnership between the United States, Japan, India, and Australia. The Quad coordinates on maritime security, technology, and infrastructure in the Indo-Pacific region, but unlike NATO, it does not include a mutual defense promise.

Military Alliance

Beyond NATO, the U.S. has separate one-on-one defense arrangements with countries around the world — though these agreements vary widely in how formal or binding they are. Four examples:

  • Israel: The U.S. has no formal mutual defense treaty with Israel, but the two countries have built a deep security relationship through agreements like the 1952 Mutual Defense Assistance Agreement and billions of dollars in annual military aid.
  • Saudi Arabia: In November 2025, the U.S. and Saudi Arabia signed a Strategic Defense Agreement and Washington granted Saudi Arabia Major Non-NATO Ally status, deepening security ties without making it a full treaty ally like Japan or South Korea.
  • Jordan: Jordan has been a Major Non-NATO Ally since 1996, and the two countries maintain a Defense Cooperation Agreement and Status of Forces Agreement rather than a mutual defense treaty.
  • Taiwan: The U.S. ended its formal Mutual Defense Treaty with Taiwan in 1980, and today provides defense support instead through the Taiwan Relations Act, which commits the U.S. to supplying Taiwan with defensive arms without a guarantee of military intervention.
CN

China

Flexible partnerships and multilateral organizations

Military Alliance

Signed in 1961, this is China's only formal mutual defense treaty with any country. It commits China to help North Korea if North Korea is attacked. China has suggested this promise would not apply if North Korea started the fight, which shows how carefully China limits even its one true military alliance.

Regional Organization

A regional group including China, Russia, India, and several Central and South Asian countries. The SCO focuses on counterterrorism, security cooperation, and trade — it is not a mutual defense alliance like NATO, and members are not required to defend one another militarily.

Economic Initiative

A large network of infrastructure and investment agreements — roads, ports, railways, and energy projects — that China has signed with roughly 150 countries. It builds influence through money and construction rather than military bases or defense promises.

Economic Bloc

An economic and political bloc that began with Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, and has since expanded to include countries such as Egypt, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates. BRICS coordinates on trade and development finance and offers members an alternative to Western-led financial institutions, but it carries no military defense obligations.
